Welcome! I am an incoming Assistant Professor in Global and Area Studies, School of Humanities and Social Science at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen. I received my dual-title Ph.D. in Political Science and Social Data Analytics (SoDA) from The Pennsylvania State University in 2026. My research develops a new framework for understanding trade politics by identifying supply chain-based political cleavages among firms. I investigate how global supply chains shape corporate responses to transnational regulations, especially in the areas of climate governance and economic sanctions, using firm-level data and text-as-data techniques. Additionally, I work on computational methods, with a focus on deep learning for semantic stance analysis.


I was on the Milton B. Dolinger Graduate Fellowship provided by the College of Liberal Arts at Penn State University (2024-2025). Previously, I was awarded the Program in Empirical International Relations (PEIR) Fellowship at Penn State (2023-2024). My research is supported by multiple research grants and awards by Penn State. I received a B.Soc.Sc in Government and International Studies from Hong Kong Baptist University, where I received a full scholarship. I also received an M.Phil. in Politics and Public Administration from the University of Hong Kong.
